# Mouse Ramp — MVP

Built 2026-10-23 to Jack's spec. A Flappy-simple, one-input game starring the footless mouse.

## Play it
`mouse-game-play.html` — fully standalone (sprites embedded), open it on the phone. `index.html` + the `mouse-*.png` sprites are the editable version.

## The game (as specified)
- **Static screen for the MVP** — no scrolling yet. Post-MVP: infinite vertical auto-scroll that starts once the mouse reaches mid-screen (conditions to be set then).
- The mouse **always runs**. He starts at the base running left and right, turning at the walls.
- **Finger down → he runs to the finger.**
- **Fingertip blocking him → he bumps it and turns the other way.** (After a bump, that press stops steering him until you lift or move the finger well away — otherwise he'd jitter against it.)
- **Finger laid down at an angle → it's a ramp.** If he can reach its low edge he runs onto it, follows it up, and steps off onto any ledge its far edge touches. Ramps bridge the gaps between platforms — that's the whole game: help him scale the ledges to the **cheese on the top ledge**.
- **Finger lifted → the line stays** (drawn dimmer) until he runs it off or you draw a new one; if he was on it he keeps climbing it instead of falling.
- Walking off a ledge end (not at a wall) he falls to the surface below, still running the same way.

## Level
Ground + 4 staggered rows of horizontal ledges, multiple per row, every in-row gap ≥ ~100px (wide enough for the mouse to pass through). Row spacing follows Jack's 1–3 inch rule as far as one static screen allows: fitted to ≈100–190px so all rows + finish fit a phone screen. Full 1–3" spacing returns with the scrolling version, where rows aren't screen-bound.

## The finger, honestly
A phone reports a touch point plus an approximate patch size/shape (`radiusX / radiusY / rotationAngle` where the browser exposes them) — but see Fix 3 below: on Jack's iPhone those values carry no orientation at all, so the game's primary ramp input is the touch's *path*. A travelled touch = ramp along its path (ramps steeper than ~70° don't count — lay the finger flatter); a big or clearly elongated still patch = ramp; a small round patch = fingertip blocker. The reported patch is only the contact core, so the ramp segment is extended along the finger's axis to a usable length. On a desktop there's no patch, so **press-and-drag draws the ramp** between press point and cursor; a plain click is a fingertip.

One behaviour note: once he steps off a ramp onto a ledge, that same held finger won't immediately re-ramp him (it kept yo-yoing him back down). Lift it, or slide it to the next gap — moving it re-arms it, which is what you'd do anyway.

### Ramp fix 3 — 2026-10-23 (measured, from Jack's Touch Lab screenshot)
His phone's actual numbers: `rotationAngle` **0.0° always**, `radiusY` **0.0**, pointer contact a square **97×97** — the patch ellipse carries *no* orientation on his device, so every diagonal finger was being read as a horizontal bar. Also confirmed: `touchstart` fires before `pointerdown` there (Fix 1's cache was necessary), and his natural diagonal lay travels at **~58° slope** (250px from (307,521) to (176,309)) — steeper than the old 55° climb limit, so even a correct segment was rejected. Fix: the touch path now **always** wins over the patch when the touch has travelled, and the climbable limit is **70°**. Regression tests T11/T12 replay his exact values (60° lay, radiusX 60.5 / radiusY 0 / rotationAngle 0, event order and all) — the mouse mounts and climbs. Touch Lab was re-synced to the same rules.

### Ramp fix 2 — 2026-10-23 (still failing on Jack's phone)
The ellipse-based fix didn't land on his device, so the primary ramp input no longer depends on patch orientation data at all: **a touch that moves defines the ramp from its own path** (touch at one end, slide a short way along the ramp, hold — laying a finger down naturally drags the touch point from fingertip toward hand, tracing the same line). The trajectory segment wins whenever it makes a runnable slope; the patch ellipse remains as a fallback for a perfectly still lay. Also in the folder: **`touch-lab.html`**, a diagnostic page that prints exactly what the phone reports for a laid finger (radiusX/Y, rotationAngle, pointer width/height, event order, and the game's own verdict) — sent to Jack for a screenshot so the next fix, if one is needed, is based on his device's real numbers rather than another inference.

### Ramp fix — 2026-10-23 (Jack: "diagonal finger doesn't become a ramp")
Three stacked causes, all fixed: (1) iOS can fire `touchstart` *before* `pointerdown`, and the original handler dropped the patch when the press hadn't registered yet — a still-held finger stayed a round fingertip forever. The patch is now cached on `touchstart` and merged into the press whenever it lands. (2) The ramp cutoff (patch radius ≥ 48) sat above what a real diagonal finger pad often reports; classification now uses elongation ratio as well as size, with the segment extended to finger length. (3) The step-up tolerance (26px) was thinner than a finger; it's now ~54px, mounting is judged at the ramp point nearest his body, and stepping onto a ramp always sends him along it toward its far end. Testing also caught two knock-on bugs — a ledge step-off instantly re-mounting him back down the same ramp (now one climb per press, above), and a fall that could start below ground level and never land (the ground is now an absolute floor).

## Random row generator + no-win stress test — 2026-10-26
`stress_test.py` (run: `python3 stress_test.py`). Jack's n / N / x model, implemented and tested — not yet wired into the game itself.

- **Rule:** each generated row is accepted only if its worst-case travel n_i ≤ x_max,i = s·h_i/a (s = 138 px/s fixed mouse speed, a = 120 px/s fixed scroll, h_i = that row's spacing, 96–288 px). Worst case = the mouse arrives through the gap that maximises its travel to the next row. Gaps ≥ 100 px wide, platform segments ≥ 60 px, ramp footprint h_i/tan(70°) counted in travel. A row that fails is regenerated (fallback: repeat the previous row's gaps, which always passes).
- **Result, 5000 capped rows, worst-case mouse:** 0 deaths. Mean travel N = 77.4 px, max travel 149.2 px, 0 fallbacks, first random row accepted ~99.9% of the time — the cap is a tail guard on extreme layouts, not a fight with typical random rows.
- **Controls (the test has teeth):** uncapped random rows also survived 5000 rows — typical random layouts are usually kind. But an adversarial layout (gap alternating far-left/far-right, h = 120, travel 170 > x_max 138) kills the mouse at row 16, and a burst of 9 hard rows followed by 100 easy rows kills at row 9 even though the full-run average travel (46.1 px) sits well below x_max (110 px). So the safe rule is the per-row cap (or a prefix average with buffer), never a whole-run average alone.
- **Note:** the per-row cap makes survival near-tautological (each row's climb rate ≥ scroll speed by construction); the test's real job is verifying the generator enforces it over thousands of rows and that random rows stay varied while doing so.

## Endless version — 2026-10-26
`mouse-game-endless-play.html` is the standalone to open on the phone (sprites embedded); `endless.html` + the `mouse-*.png` sprites are the editable version. Built on the MVP engine — same finger, ramps, and mouse physics.

- **Scroll:** once the mouse first reaches mid-screen, the frame scrolls at a fixed **120 px/s** (the stress-test's `a`, with his fixed run speed `s` = 138). After that he can never rise above the middle — the view translates to keep him there — and if he falls below the bottom of the screen, it's over (score = rows climbed, shown in the HUD, game-over banner on death, Reset restarts).
- **Rows:** generated on the fly with the capped generator above (gaps ≥ 100 px, segments ≥ 60 px, spacing 96–288 px, worst travel ≤ s·h/a, fallback repeats the previous row). New rows are generated while the topmost row is still 400 px above the screen, so there's always lookahead to draw for; rows below the screen are pruned.
- **One honest deviation from the stress-test model:** scroll **pauses while he's on a ramp**. A held finger is screen-fixed terrain, so scrolling under it would detach the ramp from its ledges mid-climb. Pausing only ever helps him, so the no-win proof still holds. Also from the MVP: the ground-as-absolute-floor catch now only applies while the ground is still on screen — once it has scrolled off, a mouse below the screen dies instead of being teleported back onto invisible ground.
- **Verification:** JS syntax-checked (editable + standalone), plus a Node harness driving the real game script with a stubbed canvas: 5000 generated rows with 0 cap violations, 0 fallbacks, min gap 100 px, min segment 60 px; scroll activates at mid-screen, the world shifts at scroll speed, and falling below the screen ends the game. No headless-browser or on-device play test yet — on-device feel is still Jack's call.

## Line rules — 2026-10-26 (Jack's rules, in both the MVP and endless)
- **Commitment:** one press is one line. Once the mouse runs off a line, that press can never mount a ramp again — sliding the finger no longer re-arms it, and a falling mouse can't land on a spent line either. He never runs back down a line; a new line needs a new press (lift and touch again).
- **Crossing:** a drawn line (drag or slide) is no longer capped at 380 px — it runs as long as drawn. But it cannot cross a platform: if the line runs through a platform ahead of him (other than the one he mounted from), he does not pass through it. At the crossing he drops vertically, x locked, to the next platform below, skipping the crossed one (if none, he falls to the floor — or to his death in endless). The ground is excluded from this (floor logic already handles it). A still-laid finger's line stays finger-length by nature — it's inferred from the finger, not drawn.
- **Verification:** a Node harness driving the real game script proves all four: he mounts, steps off, and after the finger slides he stays on the upper ledge (no re-mount, no descent); a line drawn through a middle platform drops him vertically from the crossing x to the ledge below; drag and slide lines reach past 380 px; and a falling mouse falls through a spent line. The earlier endless harness still passes unchanged.

### Fix 2026-10-26 (Jack: "isn't running off the ramp, sometimes runs back onto it")
Root cause: the commitment rule had a hole. The line was spent only when he ran off the far end or hit a crossing — but if he came off the ramp by falling (the line flickers, steepens past the limit, or shifts under him), the line stayed unspent, so he climbed straight back onto it: off, back on, never completing the run. Fix: **any** departure from a ramp spends the line, including falling off it. His direction is also now locked at mount (`rampDir`, restored every frame on the ramp), so nothing can turn him mid-traversal. Trade-off to know: if a line wobbles under him and drops him, that line is done — he won't climb back on it, you redraw. Verified with a flicker reproduction: mount, steepen the line until he falls, restore the line — he stays on the ledge and never re-mounts.

### Kept lines + edge snap — 2026-10-26 (Jack's asks; kept lines SUPERSEDE vanish-on-lift)
- **Lines stay after lifting.** A line no longer vanishes when the finger lifts — it stays on screen (drawn dimmer) and he keeps climbing it if he's on it, or can mount it later if he hasn't reached it yet. It disappears only when he runs it off (spent) or when a newly drawn line replaces it. In endless, a kept line scrolls with the world like any other terrain. One consequence: while a kept line is on screen, a plain tap won't act as a blocker — the kept line is the current line until a new line is drawn.
- **Lines disappear on disembarking — even with the finger still down.** The earlier kept-line build had a gap: after he stepped off, a still-held finger kept its live line drawn. Now a spent press is fully inert (no line, no blocker) until lift. Full lifecycle: draw → (lift: line stays) → he runs it off → line gone → lift + new press for the next line. Verified: step off with the finger held and the line is gone, the press does nothing further, and a new press after lift draws again.

### Mount requires the line to touch his platform — 2026-10-26 (Jack: when the line is drawn not connected to the platform the mouse is on, the mouse should fall)
The other half of "fully connected": a line is only usable if it touches the platform the mouse is on — the segment must pass through that platform's top edge or face (top down to top+14) at an x within both the segment's span and the platform's span. A line that merely floats near his platform (even within the old 54 px mount tolerance) or sits on another platform is not mountable: he runs past it and falls. The same gate covers falling onto a line (a fall from his platform can't land on a line that doesn't touch that platform — otherwise the mount gate could be bypassed by falling onto the line) and the green prediction (an unmountable line can't be green). Touching lines mount and climb exactly as before. Note: while the finger is still held, steering still pulls him toward the line (existing behaviour for any unmountable line); he falls once he runs past it or the finger lifts. Verified: floating-within-tolerance, other-platform, and under-the-fall-path lines all fall to the ground; touching lines mount; all prior harnesses still pass. (Tooling note: the /tmp harnesses were wiped, so the builder and all suites were rebuilt durably under tests/ — run_all.sh runs them.)

### Pointing again no longer erases the line — 2026-10-26 (Jack: when I draw a line, if I point on the screen again, it vanishes)
Two vanish paths, both fixed. (1) There was no pointer tracking, so a second finger's touch replaced the first finger outright — and since the kept line is cleared while a line is live, the line disappeared completely. Now the first finger wins: second pointers and touches are ignored entirely (down, move, and up), with a rescue if an id is ever stuck with no finger down. (2) A fresh tap with a slightly oval patch produced a patch segment that instantly replaced a kept line before any real line was drawn. Rule (corrected 2026-10-26, see below): a still point never replaces a kept line — only point+drag does.

### Front-line rule: mount only at the foot, from the largest-angle side — 2026-10-26 (Jack: a line created in front of the mouse won't change its trajectory or location; instead it runs toward the side with the largest angle to the platform beneath it (it must be touching a surface) and mounts the line)
A line drawn in front no longer steers him or snaps him: he keeps running straight, and mounts only at the line's foot — where it meets the surface he's on (new helper segFoot: the crossing point, the nearer endpoint within step distance, or the end he meets first for a flat line) — and only from the side where it makes the largest angle with that surface (the foot between him and the far end). Approaching from the high/acute side, or mid-line, he runs past instead of jumping onto it; passing the foot puts him on the largest-angle side, where he mounts and climbs back toward the high end (so a down-slope is climbed from its foot, not slid down from its top). The green prediction was updated to match: the foot itself must be reachable while supported (with steering he may turn back to a foot on his ledge; without it the foot must lie ahead in his running direction). Verified: up-slope mounts at the foot with no height snap and no direction change, then lands above; down-slope and crossing lines are not mounted from the acute side even where the old engine mounted (within 54px), then mount from the largest-angle side with no snap; all prior suites still pass.

### Delete badge: a circled x on the kept line — 2026-10-26 (Jack: could there be a small "x" in a circle on a line? When the user taps it, the line disappears)
The kept line (the one that stays after you lift) now carries a small white circled x at its midpoint. Tapping it deletes the line at once; tapping anywhere else on or near the line leaves it alone (28px hit area around the 13px badge). It only appears on the kept line — a live line is under your finger, so a second finger couldn't tap it anyway (and second fingers are ignored). Tapping the x while he climbs the line deletes it under him, so he falls; tapping the x and then dragging draws a new line instead (the delete press becomes a normal draw once it moves). Verified: badge tap deletes at once and stays deleted; off-badge tap keeps the line; mid-climb delete drops him; x-tap then drag draws and keeps a new line; all suites pass.

### Point vs point+drag, and the teleport — 2026-10-26 (Jack: if I point, the line disappears; if I point and drag, a line is created; drawing a line anywhere makes the mouse appear there)
Clarified rule, superseding the ~200 ms commit from the previous section: a point — tap or held lay, i.e. a still finger — NEVER replaces a kept line, no matter how long it is held. Only movement (drag/traj) replaces it. So a point leaves the line alone, and point+drag creates a line. (A still finger can still draw the FIRST line when nothing is kept; it just can't overwrite a kept one — to draw a new line while one is kept, drag.) Separately, the "mouse appears there" bug: when a new press replaced the line he was climbing, the engine followed the NEW segment's height at his x — an instant vertical snap onto a line drawn anywhere. Now the engine tracks which finger he mounted with (rampFinger): if a new press actually changes the line under him, he falls instead of snapping (the new press is not spent, so its line stays usable and he may land on it by falling). A lift (no finger), a morph (same finger), or a press that changed nothing (tap/point while on a kept line) keeps him climbing. Verified: point (tap or held) never replaces the kept line; drag replaces it at once; new line while climbing drops him to his ledge instead of teleporting; all suites pass.

### Correction: "connected" at the start end means within a step, not exact touch — 2026-10-26 (Jack: now it's never green)
Exact touch was too strict for finger drawing: a press lands on the mouse's body, up to a finger's width above the platform line, not pixel-perfect on it — so no real line ever counted as touching and green never lit (and mounting would have failed the same way). Start-connection is now defined as stepping distance: the segment's y-range over the overlap of its span and his platform's span must meet [top−54, top+54] (the climb tolerance mounting itself uses). Lines with no horizontal overlap with his platform at all (another platform, or past his platform's edge) or farther than a step away vertically still fall and still can't be green. The far end stays strict (top edge or face only). Verified: a 40 px float over his platform is green; a 67–100 px float is amber; no-overlap lines still fall; all suites pass.

### Green redefined: fully connected only — 2026-10-26 (Jack: the line turns green even if it isn't fully connected)
Green used to mean "he'll end up on a platform somehow," which lit for three visibly unconnected cases: a snap gap short of an edge (up to 48 px), a drop (line ending in mid-air high above a platform), and a crossing drop (line never reaches its far end). With the outline gone those gaps are plain to see, so green now means the line is FULLY CONNECTED: it can be mounted, no platform blocks it ahead, and its far end actually touches a platform — within the platform's span, on its top edge or its face. Snap, drop, and crossing-drop lines now show amber. Note the meaning change: amber no longer means "will fall" — snap/drop lines still land, they just aren't fully connected; only lines that miss everything fall. The engine's forgiving landings are untouched; only the color's promise changed. Verified: snap-gap, drop-gap, above-top-gap, and crossing lines are amber; top-edge, face, face-bottom-edge, and edge-touching lines are green; all prior harnesses still pass.

### No outline: the visible line is the logical line — 2026-10-26 (Jack: the line has a thick outline, remove it)
The line was drawn as two strokes — a 26 px outer stroke with round caps plus a 16 px inner one. Those round caps overhung the true endpoints by 13 px, so a line could look connected to a platform while its logical end fell short — the visual/logical mismatch behind several "but it looked connected" reports. Now it's a single 16 px stroke with flat (butt) ends: the visible line starts and ends exactly where the engine's segment does. Green/amber and kept-dimmer coloring are unchanged, just on the single stroke. Physics untouched; all harnesses still pass.

### Face counts as connecting — 2026-10-26 (Jack's ruling on the "connecting two platforms" question)
Step-on now accepts a far end on the platform's visible face: the landing window is 40 px above the top surface down through the 14 px face (CFG.faceH, matching the drawn platform rect). Only a line ending *below* the face — pointed at the underside from below — still falls instead of mounting. One subtlety this required: a downward line ending on a face would otherwise trip the crossing rule (it crosses the platform's top plane on the way down), so the crossing check now exempts a platform the line terminates on (far end in travel direction on its face, within edgeSnap) — such a line connects to it, it doesn't run through it. The green prediction mirrors both changes, so face-ending lines are green. Verified: upward face endings mount (including exactly at the face bottom edge, 14 px below the top); downward face endings land with no crossing drop; downward below-face endings still drop past the target to the ground; the old crossing test (line through a platform, far end elsewhere) is unaffected; all prior harnesses still pass. This supersedes the old B1 expectation (endpoint inside the body fell) and the strict "at or above the top" wording — the underside rule now starts below the face.

### Green fixed: it must also be mountable — 2026-10-26 (Jack: green lines didn't guarantee it either)
The first green prediction had a real hole: it asked "if he runs this line, does he land?" but never asked "can he get on it?" The engine only mounts a line while he's running, within 54 px vertically and 35 px horizontally of his path, and before his ledge runs out under him — so a line floating too high, past his ledge's edge, behind him on a kept line (no steering once the finger lifts), or drawn while falling all showed green while he ran off his ledge and fell without ever touching the line. The prediction now requires an actual mount: mode must be running (on-ramp counts as mounted), and some point on the line within his ledge's reachable interval must sit within mount height of his ledge — with steering (live line, finger held) either approach counts, without it only his current running direction counts, and a sloping line whose middle is in reach counts as mountable. Verified: floating-high, behind-him-kept, past-ledge-edge, falling, and too-low lines are all amber now; reachable, mid-segment-mount, and on-ramp lines stay green; all previous green tests still pass. Remaining honest limit: green means he mounts the line and lands on a platform — not that he stays there (he always keeps running), and in endless a drop target very near the screen bottom could in principle scroll away mid-fall; if a green line still drops him without him ever touching a platform, that setup is the next bug to chase.

### Green = guaranteed landing — 2026-10-26 (Jack: mouse still falls off when the line runs out; turn the line green if it guarantees landing on a platform)
The line now predicts its own outcome and turns **green** when running it is guaranteed to end on a platform, staying amber otherwise. The prediction mirrors the engine's rules in engine order, so it never promises what the physics won't do: (1) a line too steep to climb is never green; (2) a line running through a platform is green only if a platform below the crossing catches the vertical drop (the crossed platform itself never counts, and ground never counts); (3) otherwise the far-end exit is green if a platform takes him by step-on (at/above its top, within the 48 px edge tolerance) or by the vertical drop onto the nearest platform below the far end. Kept lines predict too, at their dimmer green. Green means "running this line lands him on a platform" — not "he will necessarily run it" (e.g. if he's running away from it). It also doubles as a diagnostic for any remaining fall report: a fall off a green line is a real bug with an exact setup to chase; a fall off an amber line is the indicator working as intended. Verified: step-on, drop, crossing-with-platform-below, and kept lines all green; bottom-point over open air, open air, unclimbable, and crossing-with-nothing-below all amber — including the ordering case where a far-end drop must not rescue a crossing that drops to ground.

### No mounting from below — 2026-10-26 (Jack: line pointed to the bottom of a platform, mouse should fall, not mount it)
Root cause: the far-end step-on accepted a platform within 40 px in *either* direction, so a line ending under a platform's underside teleported him up through its body onto the top. Step-on is now one-sided: it only counts when the line ends at or above the platform's top surface (up to 40 px above, stepping down onto it). A line ending below the top level — inside the platform's body or under its bottom face — mounts nothing; he falls instead (dropping onto whatever is below, per the drop rule). Lines ending exactly at the top level or above it still mount as before. Verified: lines ending inside the body and 12 px below the bottom face both fall back without ever mounting; at-level and above-level controls still mount. (One test caught its own bug along the way: the first version of this harness "passed" without the mouse ever mounting the ramp, because it stopped the moment he was still standing on the lower ledge — it now waits for a real mount first.)

### Drop onto the platform below — 2026-10-26 (Jack: line ends above a platform, mouse falls and misses it)
Root cause: a far end more than 40 px above a platform didn't count as a step-on, so he fell — and falling keeps his horizontal run going, so while dropping he drifted sideways past the platform he was over. Whether he landed depended on the gap size and how close to the edge he started, hence "sometimes". Fix: if the far end is over a platform (within the same 48 px edge tolerance) but too far above it to step on, he now drops **vertically** (x locked, same mechanism as the crossing drop) onto the nearest platform below the far end, landing on its nearest edge if the far end is just short of it. Far ends over open air still fall normally with drift. Verified: a far end 80 px above a 50 px-wide platform lands dead centre on it (the old fall would have drifted ~40 px and missed); a far end 10 px short of an edge drops onto the edge; a far end over open air falls normally to the ground.
- **Edge snap (`edgeSnap` = 48 px).** Running off a ramp's far end now climbs onto a platform if the far end is over it, or within 48 px of its edge (nearest edge wins, vertical distance breaks ties; the vertical tolerance stays 40 px). Near misses that used to drop him now land him on the edge instead. 48 px is a tuning knob — say the word if you want it looser or tighter.
- **Verification:** new harness tests prove a far end 20 px short of an edge snaps on (landing on the edge) while 60 px short falls back; a line kept after a mid-climb lift completes onto the ledge and then clears; a line kept after lifting before mounting is still mountable and climbable; and his direction stays locked at 1 through a mid-climb line morph. All previous harnesses still pass.

### Green = lands — 2026-10-27 (Jack: "landing lines should be green")
Reverses the 2026-10-26 "fully connected only" definition. The complaint was that amber lines still disembarked: a snap gap (up to 48 px short of an edge), a drop (far end high above a platform), and a step-up (far end up to 40 px above the top) all landed via the exit's tolerances while showing amber. Now green means the far end LANDS — exact touch, edge snap, step-up, or drop — implemented as a shared `exitLanding(endX, endY)` helper used by BOTH the engine's far-edge exit and the green prediction, so the indicator can never disagree with what the exit actually does. Behavior is untouched (the exit's plat/drop search moved verbatim into the helper); only the color's promise changed. Scope: crossing lines stay amber — they drop mid-line and never reach the far end, even though the crossing drop lands them below. Amber now means the far end does not land (falls off the end, or never mounts). Verified: G2 (drop), N1 (snap), N3 (step-up) flipped from amber to green; G3 (below-face), G4 (open air), G6/G7 (crossing), and all reachability/face/drop/disembark/stay/flicker/line-rules/endless/point/delete/front suites still pass; new `test_green_land.js` proves each landing path predicts green AND actually lands when run; both standalones rebuilt.
